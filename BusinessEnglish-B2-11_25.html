UNIT 1 - Workplace Culture & Fairness

to treat someone fairly
/truːt ˈsʌmwʌn ˈfeəli/
To handle someone with justice and equality, giving them the same respect and consideration as others.
A good manager treats all team members fairly regardless of their background or position.
to be on a salary of
/biː ɒn ə ˈsæl.ə.ri ɒv/
To earn a fixed amount of money per year as compensation for work.
She is on a salary of £45,000 per year for her position as a marketing manager.
to be at a pay rate
/biː æt ə peɪ reɪt/
To earn money at a specified amount per hour, day, or other time period.
The new hire is at a pay rate of £15 per hour for the first three months.
to establish an equal pay structure
/ɪˈstæb.lɪʃ ən ˈiː.kwəl peɪ ˈstrʌk.tʃər/
To create a system where people are paid fairly and equally for doing the same work, regardless of gender or background.
The company needs to establish an equal pay structure to ensure everyone earns the same salary for the same job.
to be an incentive
/biː ən ɪnˈsen.tɪv/
To serve as a motivation or reason for someone to work harder or perform better.
A bonus scheme can be an incentive for employees to reach their sales targets.
to rise in a company hierarchy
/raɪz ɪn ə ˈkʌm.pən.i haɪˈɑː.rə.ki/
To move up to higher and more important positions in a company with greater responsibility.
He rose in the company hierarchy from junior developer to chief technology officer in ten years.
to bring value to the company
/brɪŋ ˈvæl.juː tə ðə ˈkʌm.pən.i/
To contribute something useful and beneficial that helps the company succeed and improves its performance.
Every team member should strive to bring value to the company through their skills and dedication.
organisational behaviour
/ˌɔː.ɡən.aɪˈzeɪ.ʃən.əl bɪˈheɪ.vjər/
The study of how people behave and interact in organizations, including their attitudes, motivation, and group dynamics.
Understanding organisational behaviour helps managers create better teams and improve workplace productivity.
a good atmosphere
/ə ɡʊd ˈæt.məs.fɪər/
A positive, pleasant, and comfortable environment at work where people feel happy and safe.
The company has created a good atmosphere where employees feel valued and motivated.
to develop a strategy for something
/dɪˈvel.əp ə ˈstræt.ə.dʒi fɔː ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To create a detailed plan or approach to achieve a particular goal or objective.
The HR team will develop a strategy for improving employee retention and reducing staff turnover.
to affect the company structure
/əˈfekt ðə ˈkʌm.pən.i ˈstrʌk.tʃər/
To cause changes in how a company is organized, including changes to departments, roles, and reporting relationships.
The merger will affect the company structure, requiring significant reorganization of departments.
to enhance the image of the company
/ɪnˈhɑːns ðə ˈɪm.ɪdʒ əv ðə ˈkʌm.pən.i/
To improve how people perceive or think about the company; to make it appear more professional and positive.
Implementing sustainability practices will enhance the image of the company with customers and investors.
to have an open-plan office
/həv ən ˈəʊ.pən plæn ˈɒf.ɪs/
To have a workspace with few walls or physical barriers where multiple employees work together in one large area.
Our company has an open-plan office to encourage collaboration and communication between teams.
to have flexibility in
/həv ˈflek.sɪ.bɪl.ə.ti ɪn/
To have the ability to adapt or change something; to have freedom in how, where, or when you do something.
The company offers flexibility in working arrangements so employees can work from home when needed.
menial tasks
/ˈmiː.ni.əl tɑːsks/
Simple, routine work that does not require special skills or training; repetitive low-level work.
Even senior managers sometimes do menial tasks to stay connected with daily operations.
diverse work
/daɪˈvɜːs wɜːk/
A variety of different types of tasks and activities in a job; work that is varied and not repetitive.
This position offers diverse work including meetings, project management, and client interaction.
on sabbatical leave
/ɒn səˈbæt.ɪ.kəl liːv/
A long break from work while keeping your job, typically taken to rest, study, travel, or pursue other interests.
She took sabbatical leave for six months to complete her master's degree.
to get a promotion
/ɡet ə prəˈməʊ.ʃən/
To move to a higher position with more responsibility and usually higher salary.
After five years of excellent performance, she finally got a promotion to senior manager.
work-life balance
/wɜːk laɪf ˈbæl.əns/
The division of time and energy fairly between work and personal life, family, and leisure activities.
Good companies prioritize work-life balance to prevent employee burnout and stress.
to retain employees
/rɪˈteɪn ɪmˈplɔɪ.iːz/
To keep employees working at a company; to prevent valued staff from leaving the organization.
The company offers competitive salaries and benefits to retain their best employees.
high/low turnover
/haɪ / ləʊ ˈtɜː.nəʊ.vər/
The rate at which employees leave a company (high turnover means many leave; low turnover means few leave).
The company has low turnover because employees are happy and feel valued in their roles.
blue-collar workers
/ˈbluː ˌkɒl.ər ˈwɜː.kərz/
Workers who perform manual or industrial labor, typically in manufacturing, construction, or trade jobs.
Blue-collar workers in the manufacturing sector have seen significant changes due to automation.
white-collar workers
/ˈwaɪt ˌkɒl.ər ˈwɜː.kərz/
Professional or office workers who perform administrative or intellectual work, typically in business settings.
White-collar workers often benefit from flexible working arrangements and remote work options.
pink-collar workers
/ˈpɪŋk ˌkɒl.ər ˈwɜː.kərz/
Workers in service and care professions, such as nursing, teaching, childcare, and social services.
Pink-collar workers play a vital role in healthcare and education but often face lower wages than other sectors.
black-collar workers
/ˈblæk ˌkɒl.ər ˈwɜː.kərz/
Informal or illegal workers who operate outside the formal economy; workers involved in criminal activities or undocumented work.
Black-collar workers often lack legal protections and fair working conditions.
to perk someone up
/tə pɜːk ˈsʌm.wʌn ʌp/
To make someone feel happier, more energetic, or more interested; to improve someone's mood or energy.
A small pay raise or recognition can perk employees up and boost their motivation.
to nail something down
/teɪl ˈsʌm.θɪŋ daʊn/
To finalize or confirm something; to establish details firmly so that they cannot be changed.
We need to nail down the project budget and timeline before we start the work.
to be accountable for
/biː əˈkaʊnt.ə.bəl fɔː/
To be responsible for something; to be required to explain and justify your actions or results.
I am accountable for the result of the classes I conduct. She is accountable only to the managing director.
to concentrate on something/someone
/ˈkɒn.sən.treɪt ɒn/
To focus your full attention and effort on one particular task, person, or area.
We need to concentrate on tasks and concentrate on developing a new product.
to be empowered to do something
/biː ɪmˈpaʊəd tə duː ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To be given the legal right, authority, confidence, and resources to make decisions and take action.
I'm empowered to jettison any sub-standard products. Employees are empowered to make decisions without always asking their manager.
to entail something
/ɪnˈteɪl ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To involve, include, or mean as a necessary consequence; to require something as part of a process.
This entails a lot of talking, mailing, and getting on people's cases. Implementing this technology entails spending a lot of money.
to get on top of things
/ɡet ɒn tɒp əv θɪŋz/
To become fully informed or up-to-date about something; to take control of a situation and manage it effectively.
Right on track! Jen got on top of it while you were on vacation.
to take (something) on board
/teɪk ɒn bɔːrd/
To take into consideration and use an idea or suggestion; to accept and apply something.
I will take on board all the useful tricks that I will learn at the conference and use them in my work.
to take on a task
/teɪk ɒn ə tɑːsk/
To accept responsibility for and begin completing a particular job or assignment.
Who is going to complete the task? - I will take it on.

UNIT 2 - Technology & Software Development

installment
/ɪnˈstɔːl.mənt/
A single payment for something that is being paid for in multiple payments over a period of time; a part of a series or sequence.
You can purchase the software in monthly installments rather than paying the full cost upfront.
to carry out
/ˈkær.i aʊt/
To perform or complete a task; to execute a plan or activity.
The team will carry out extensive testing before the software release.
to come up with
/kʌm ʌp wɪð/
To think of or create an idea or solution; to produce or develop something.
We need to come up with a better algorithm to optimize database queries.
to pay off
/peɪ ɒf/
To result in success or benefit; to have a positive outcome after effort or investment.
Learning Python really paid off when I started working in data analysis.
to take up
/teɪk ʌp/
To begin or start doing something; to occupy space or time.
I'm not very good at golf – I only took it up recently. / Cloud storage takes up less physical space than traditional servers.
to bring down
/brɪŋ daʊn/
To cause to fail or stop working; to reduce or lower something.
A DDoS attack can bring down an entire website within minutes. / They've really brought down the price of electric cars.
to bring about
/brɪŋ əˈbaʊt/
To cause something to happen; to create or produce a result or change.
Artificial intelligence has brought about major changes in software development.
to take forward
/teɪk ˈfɔː.wərd/
To continue developing or progressing with something; to move a project or idea to the next stage.
Management decided to take forward the mobile app project to the next phase.
to get round
/ɡet raʊnd/
To find a solution to a problem; to avoid an obstacle or difficulty.
The developers found a way to get round the compatibility issues, the bug, the restrictions, and the problem.
to set up
/set ʌp/
To install, configure, or establish something; to put something in place and prepare it for use.
It took two hours to set up the new server environment.
to have a mastery of
/həv ə ˈmæs.tər.i əv/
To have complete knowledge, skill, and expertise in something; to have thoroughly learned and understood a subject or technique.
The senior developer has a mastery of multiple programming languages and frameworks.
to be embedded
/biː ɪmˈbed.ɪd/
To be placed or fixed firmly in something; to be integrated or incorporated as part of something larger.
The new safety features are embedded in the latest version of the software application.
to accelerate the training
/əkˈsel.ə.reɪt ðə ˈtreɪ.nɪŋ/
To increase the speed or pace of learning and development; to make the training process faster or more efficient.
The company decided to accelerate the training program to prepare employees for the new system launch.
to take the lead
/teɪk ðə liːd/
To be the first to do something; to assume a position of leadership or control in a situation or project.
She decided to take the lead on the new initiative and drive the team forward.
know like the back of our hands
/nəʊ laɪk ðə bæk əv aʊə hændz/
To know something extremely well; to be very familiar with a place, subject, or skill.
The team members know the software like the back of their hands after working with it for five years.
It's your call on
/ɪts jɔː kɔːl ɒn/
It is your decision or choice about something; you have the authority to make the final decision.
It's your call on whether we proceed with the project or wait for more information.
You might not like what I'm about to say but
/juː maɪt nɒt laɪk wɒt aɪm əˈbaʊt tə seɪ bʌt/
A phrase used to warn someone before you deliver difficult, negative, or critical feedback; to prepare them for unwelcome news.
You might not like what I'm about to say but the project is behind schedule and we need to make some tough decisions.
to pick up on
/pɪk ʌp ɒn/
To notice or become aware of something; to understand something that someone has implied or suggested.
The manager quickly picked up on the tension in the team during the meeting.
makes sense
/meɪks sens/
To be logical, clear, or understandable; to be a reasonable or sensible idea.
Your proposal makes sense from a budget perspective, and the team agrees it's the right direction.

UNIT 3 - Money Issues

to put money aside for something
/pʊt ˈmʌn.i əˈsaɪd fɔː ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To save money for a specific purpose or goal in the future.
I try to put money aside for emergencies each month.
to shell out (a lot of money) for something
/ʃel aʊt ə lɒt əv ˈmʌn.i fɔː ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To spend a large amount of money on something.
We had to shell out quite a bit for the new office equipment.
to splash out on something
/splæʃ aʊt ɒn ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To spend money on something enjoyable or luxurious.
We decided to splash out on a company retreat to boost team morale.
to cut back on something
/kʌt bæk ɒn ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To reduce spending or use of something.
The company had to cut back on travel expenses during the pandemic.
to take out a mortgage
/teɪk aʊt ə ˈmɔː.ɡɪ.dʒ/
To borrow money from a bank to purchase property.
They decided to take out a mortgage to buy their first home.
to pay off a loan
/peɪ ɒf ə ləʊn/
To repay all the money borrowed.
It took her ten years to pay off the loan after completing her education.
to build a nest egg
/bɪld ə nest eɡ/
To save money gradually for future security or retirement.
By investing regularly, she has built a comfortable nest egg for retirement.
to invest in something
/ɪnˈvest ɪn ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To put money into something for profit or future benefit.
The company decided to invest in new technology to improve efficiency.
to boost something
/buːst ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To increase or improve something.
Better marketing strategies will boost our sales significantly.
the bottom line
/ðə ˈbɒt.əm laɪn/
The most important fact or the final profit or loss.
The bottom line is that we need to increase revenue to remain competitive.
to make up ground
/meɪk ʌp ɡraʊnd/
To recover lost time or progress; to catch up after falling behind.
We need to make up ground on our sales targets for this quarter.
to make a mark on something
/meɪk ə mɑːk ɒn ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To have a significant impact or leave a lasting impression.
Her innovative strategies have made a mark on the entire industry.
