UNIT 1 - Jobs & Work Roles

to work with someone
/wɜːk wɪð ˈsʌm.wʌn/
To spend time doing a job together with another person.
I work with my team to complete our projects on time.
to be responsible for smth/smb
/biː rɪˈspɒn.sə.bəl fɔː/
To have a duty to take care of something or someone; it is your job to do something.
As a manager, I am responsible for my team's success and safety.
to consist of smth/smb
/kənˈsɪst əv/
To be made up of or contain certain parts or people.
Our department consists of ten people from different backgrounds.
to be in charge of smb/smth
/biː ɪn tʃɑːdʒ əv/
To have control over someone or something; to be the person who makes decisions.
The director is in charge of the whole company.
to specialise in smth
/ˈspeʃ.ə.laɪz ɪn/
To focus your work on one particular area or skill; to become an expert in something.
She specialises in digital marketing and knows a lot about social media.
to work as a ...
/wɜːk æz ə/
To have a job in a particular position or profession.
He works as a consultant and helps companies with their business problems.
to work in ...
/wɜːk ɪn/
To do a job in a particular industry or field.
She works in finance and deals with money and budgets.
to deal with smb/smth
/diːl wɪð/
To handle something; to take action or do something about a problem or person.
I deal with customer complaints and try to solve their problems.
to tend to
/tend tə/
To usually do something or usually be something; to have a common behaviour or habit.
Senior staff tend to work longer hours than new employees.
to oversee
/ˌəʊ.vərˈsiː/
To watch over or check that something is done correctly; to manage and control something.
The project manager will oversee all the work to make sure it is good quality.
a notion
/ə ˈnəʊ.ʃən/
An idea or understanding about something; what you think something is or means.
Many people have a wrong notion of what accountants do in their job.

UNIT 2 - Work-Life Balance

to work flexitime
/wɜːk ˈflek.sɪ.taɪm/
To work flexible hours where you choose when to start and finish work, as long as you work the required total hours.
She works flexitime, so she can start at 8 am or 9 am depending on her family needs.
to work during core hours
/wɜːk ˈdjʊə.rɪŋ kɔːr aʊərz/
To work during the required time period when all employees must be in the office, usually mid-morning to mid-afternoon.
Everyone must work during core hours from 10 am to 3 pm when the office is busiest.
to do overtime, paid/unpaid overtime
/duː ˈəʊ.vər.taɪm/
To work extra hours beyond your normal working hours, either earning extra pay (paid) or without extra payment (unpaid).
During the busy season, many employees do overtime to complete all the projects.
to have a home-working system / to allow home-working
/həv ə həʊm ˈwɜː.kɪŋ ˈsɪs.təm / əˈlaʊ həʊm ˈwɜː.kɪŋ/
To have an arrangement where employees can work from home, or to permit employees to work remotely.
The company allows home-working two days a week so employees can focus on important work.
to take annual leave / to be on annual leave
/teɪk ˈæn.ju.əl liːv / biː ɒn ˈæn.ju.əl liːv/
To use your paid vacation days given each year by the company, or to be away from work using these days.
In the UK, employees typically take annual leave in summer and sometimes at Christmas.
to apply for additional leave / to be on additional leave
/əˈplaɪ fər əˈdɪʃ.ən.əl liːv / biː ɒn əˈdɪʃ.ən.əl liːv/
To request extra time off beyond your normal annual leave, or to be away from work using this extra leave.
She applied for additional leave to care for her sick mother.
to get statutory sick pay
/ɡet ˈstæt.ʃu.tər.i sɪk peɪ/
To receive money from your employer or the government when you are too ill to work.
When she was ill for a week, she got statutory sick pay so her pay did not change.
to take unpaid leave / to be on unpaid leave
/teɪk ʌnˈpeɪd liːv / biː ɒn ʌnˈpeɪd liːv/
To take time off from work without receiving pay, or to be away from work without payment.
He took unpaid leave for two months to travel around South America.
to take paternity leave / to be on paternity leave
/teɪk pəˈtɜː.nə.ti liːv / biː ɒn pəˈtɜː.nə.ti liːv/
To take paid time off when your child is born, or to be away from work for this reason.
He took paternity leave for two weeks to be with his newborn baby.
to receive full pay
/rɪˈsiːv fʊl peɪ/
To get your complete normal salary even when not working, such as during leave.
During maternity leave, she receives full pay for the first three months.
on public holidays
/ɒn ˈpʌb.lɪk ˈhɒl.ɪ.deɪz/
During official days off that the government declares when most people do not work, such as Christmas or New Year.
Banks are closed on public holidays, so you cannot do your banking then.
to take a lunch break
/teɪk ə lʌntʃ breɪk/
To stop working to eat lunch and rest during the working day.
Most employees take a lunch break at noon to eat and relax.

UNIT 3 - Projects & Deadlines

a commitment
/ə kəˈmɪt.mənt/
A promise or decision to do something; the act of deciding to devote yourself to something.
The company made a commitment to finish the project on time.
to meet the deadline
/miːt ðə ˈded.laɪn/
To complete work or deliver something by the required date and time.
We need to meet the deadline of March 31st to launch the new product.
to fall behind schedule
/fɔːl bɪˈhaɪnd ˈʃed.juːl/
To not progress as fast as planned; to be slower than expected and be late.
The project fell behind schedule because we did not have enough staff.
to catch up
/kætʃ ʌp/
To do work faster to return to the original plan; to reach the same level or progress as before.
We need to catch up on lost time by working extra hours this week.
to be back on track
/biː bæk ɒn træk/
To return to the original plan after problems; to progress as expected again.
After solving the technical issues, the project is back on track.
to finish ahead of schedule
/ˈfɪn.ɪʃ əˈhed əv ˈʃed.juːl/
To complete work earlier than originally planned; to finish before the deadline.
The team worked efficiently and finished the project ahead of schedule.
to allocate resources
/ˈæl.ə.keɪt ˈrez.ɔː.sɪz/
To distribute money, people, or equipment to different tasks or projects.
The manager will allocate resources to ensure each project has what it needs.
to stay within budget
/steɪ wɪðˈɪn ˈbʌdʒ.ɪt/
To not spend more money than the amount planned; to keep costs as planned.
We must stay within budget to avoid losing money on this project.
to delegate tasks
/ˈdel.ɪ.ɡeɪt tɑːsks/
To give work or responsibility to other people so you do not do everything yourself.
A good manager delegates tasks fairly to all team members.
to get on with a task
/ɡet ɒn wɪð ə tɑːsk/
To start working on something and make progress; to continue doing a task.
Stop chatting and get on with your work if you want to finish today.
to get updates
/ɡet ˈʌp.deɪts/
To receive the most recent information about progress or changes in a situation.
The team leader sends updates every Friday so everyone knows the project progress.

UNIT 4 - Common Expressions

it's not a rocket science
/ɪts nɒt ə ˈrɒk.ɪt ˈsaɪ.əns/
Something is not difficult to understand or do; it is easy and does not require special knowledge or skill.
Don't worry about the new software – it's not a rocket science. You'll learn it quickly.
user-friendly
/ˈjuː.zər ˈfren.dli/
Easy to use and understand; designed to be simple for people to use without difficulty.
The new software is very user-friendly and anyone can learn to use it quickly.
user-unfriendly
/ˈjuː.zər ʌnˈfren.dli/
Difficult to use and understand; not designed for people to use easily.
The old system was user-unfriendly and staff complained about it constantly.
complicated
/ˈkɒm.plɪ.keɪ.tɪd/
Complex and difficult to understand or deal with; having many parts or details that are confusing.
The instructions were too complicated, so we asked for help from an expert.
accurate
/ˈæk.jər.ət/
Correct and exact; free from mistakes or errors.
The financial report must be accurate before we send it to the board.
inaccurate
/ɪnˈæk.jər.ət/
Not correct; containing mistakes or errors.
The data in the spreadsheet was inaccurate, so we had to correct it.
secure
/sɪˈkjʊər/
Safe and protected; not able to be hacked or broken into easily.
We use a secure system to protect our customer information.
insecure
/ɪn.sɪˈkjʊər/
Not safe or protected; easily damaged or broken into.
The old system was insecure and often had problems with data loss.
high-quality
/ˈhaɪ ˈkwɒl.ə.ti/
Very good and of excellent standard; made with care and attention to detail.
We only use high-quality materials to make our products.
low-quality
/ˈləʊ ˈkwɒl.ə.ti/
Not very good; made without care and likely to break or fail easily.
The low-quality product did not last very long.
highly-qualified
/ˈhaɪ.li ˈkwɒl.ɪ.faɪd/
Having excellent skills, knowledge, and experience; well-trained and skilled.
We hired a highly-qualified engineer to lead our project.
handy
/ˈhæn.di/
Useful and convenient; easy to use and always available when needed.
This tool is very handy and I use it every day in my work.
inconvenient
/ˌɪn.kənˈviː.ni.ənt/
Not suitable or comfortable; causing trouble or difficulty.
The office location is inconvenient because it is far from the train station.
awkward
/ˈɔː.kwərd/
Difficult to use or move; uncomfortable and not smooth or easy.
The keyboard is awkward to use and tires my hands quickly.
come in handy
/kʌm ɪn ˈhæn.di/
To be useful or helpful when needed; to prove useful in a situation.
The extra training came in handy when we had to work with the new software.
up-to-date
/ˌʌp.təˈdeɪt/
Modern and current; showing the latest information or changes.
We need to keep our files up-to-date so everyone has the correct information.
outdated / out-of-date
/ˌaʊtˈdeɪ.tɪd / ˌaʊt əv ˈdeɪt/
No longer current or modern; old and no longer useful or fashionable.
The computer system is outdated and we need to replace it with new equipment.
efficient
/ɪˈfɪʃ.ənt/
Working well and quickly without wasting time or energy; producing good results with little waste.
The new process is more efficient and saves the company time and money.
inefficient
/ˌɪn.ɪˈfɪʃ.ənt/
Not working well; wasting time and energy without producing good results.
The old system was inefficient and we lost a lot of productivity.
time-consuming
/ˈtaɪm kənˈsuː.mɪŋ/
Taking a lot of time to do; requiring a long period of time to complete.
Filling out the forms is very time-consuming and takes several hours.
