B2+ Business Vocabulary

Group 8_25

UNIT 2 - Manager or Mentor

a feedback loop
/ə ˈfiːd.bæk luːp/
A system where output or results are returned as input to improve or adjust a process; a cycle of information exchange.
Establishing a feedback loop with your team helps identify areas for improvement and track progress over time.
pace of something
/peɪs əv ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
The speed or rate at which something happens or progresses.
The pace of change in the industry is accelerating rapidly. We need to match the pace of our competitors to remain competitive.
to pass on something (advice)
/tə pɑːs ɒn ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To share, transmit, or give something (such as knowledge, advice, or information) to another person.
My mentor passed on valuable advice about navigating corporate politics. She always passes on her experience to younger colleagues.
There is not room for ...
/ðeə ɪz nɒt ruːm fɔː/
There is no space, opportunity, or tolerance for something; something is not acceptable or cannot be accommodated.
There is not room for complacency in this competitive market. There is not room for error in our quality control processes.
There is always room for ...
/ðeə ɪz ˈɔː.weɪz ruːm fɔː/
There is always opportunity, space, or acceptance for something; something can always be improved or included.
There is always room for improvement in our processes. There is always room for new ideas and creative solutions.
to do something on the fly
/tə duː ˈsʌm.θɪŋ ɒn ðə flaɪ/
To do something quickly and without much preparation, usually while doing something else or while moving.
We had to modify the presentation on the fly when the projector malfunctioned. The team can make decisions on the fly during the project.
cooperative atmosphere
/kəʊˈɒp.ər.ə.tɪv ˈæt.mə.sfɪər/
An environment where people are willing to help and work together toward common goals.
A cooperative atmosphere encourages teamwork and open communication among colleagues.
mutually beneficial
/ˈmjuː.tʃu.ə.li ˌben.ɪˈfɪʃ.əl/
Advantageous or helpful to all parties involved; providing equal benefits to both or all sides.
It will be a mutually beneficial project for both companies. A mutually beneficial agreement ensures cooperation with the partner and strengthens the relationship.
to call the shots
/kɔːl ðə ʃɒts/
To make important decisions and have control or authority over a situation.
You're the boss here – you get to call the shots. The project manager calls the shots on all major decisions.
to have the best interests of someone at heart / to have someone's best interests at heart
/həv ðə best ˈɪn.trəsts əv ˈsʌm.wʌn ət hɑːt/
To genuinely care about someone's wellbeing and to act in ways that benefit them.
He claims that he has her best interests at heart. A good mentor has their mentee's interests at heart.
to be a two-way street
/biː ə tuː weɪ striːt/
A situation involving mutual responsibility, effort, or benefit; something that requires give-and-take from both parties.
Marriage is a two-way street. Cooperation with the partner is a two-way street that requires commitment from both sides. Friendship and a successful project are a two-way street.
to be interested in inclusion
/biː ˈɪn.trə.stɪd ɪn ɪnˈkluː.ʒən/
To care about ensuring that all people are welcomed, valued, and able to participate fully regardless of their backgrounds or differences.
His inclusion in the team has caused controversy but shows commitment to diversity. Being interested in inclusion means actively creating space for diverse perspectives.
a nurturing environment
/ə ˈnɜː.tʃər.ɪŋ ɪnˈvaɪ.rən.mənt/
A supportive and caring atmosphere where people can grow, develop, and feel valued and motivated.
Effective leaders strive to foster a nurturing environment where team members feel valued, supported, and motivated to grow.
to feel isolated
/fiːl ˈaɪ.sə.leɪ.tɪd/
To experience loneliness or disconnection from others; to feel separated or excluded from a group.
Remote employees may feel isolated if there is a lack of regular communication and team engagement.
to grow mistrust
/ɡrəʊ mɪsˈtrʌst/
To develop or increase a lack of confidence or faith in someone or something over time.
Inconsistent messaging from leadership can grow mistrust among employees and reduce morale. Broken promises grow mistrust within teams.
to be candid
/biː ˈkæn.dɪd/
To be honest, open, and straightforward in communication; to tell the truth, especially about something difficult or painful.
A candid talk about performance issues can help employees improve. To be candid with you, I think you're making a dreadful mistake. A candid discussion and candid response are essential for trust.
peers
/pɪərz/
People of the same age, social position, or having the same abilities as other people in a group.
She respects her professional peers and values their opinions. Despite significant peer pressure to conform, she maintained her individual beliefs and values.
can't cut it
/kɑːnt kʌt ɪt/
To not be good enough or capable enough to meet the required standard or expectations.
If the employee can't cut it in this role, we may need to consider reassignment. The new software can't cut it for our high-volume operations.
killed by kindness
/kɪld baɪ ˈkaɪnd.nəs/
Harmed or weakened by excessive generosity or leniency that results in negative consequences.
The project was killed by kindness when too many compromises were made to please everyone. A company can be killed by kindness if it offers too many benefits without maintaining profitability.
make hard calls
/meɪk hɑːrd kɔːlz/
To make difficult decisions, especially ones that are unpopular or have negative consequences.
A good manager must be prepared to make hard calls when downsizing is necessary. Leaders who make hard calls are often respected for their decisive action.
a cautionary tale
/ə ˈkɔː.ʃən.er.i teɪl/
A story or example that serves as a warning about what can go wrong or what to avoid.
The company's failure is a cautionary tale about the dangers of poor planning. His career path is a cautionary tale for anyone considering rapid expansion without proper resources.
to exude
/ɪɡˈzjuːd/
To display or show a feeling or quality in a very obvious and confident way.
A confident leader exudes authority and calm in stressful situations. She exudes professionalism and competence in every meeting.
cut the mustard
/kʌt ðə ˈmʌs.tɚd/
To be good enough or meet the required standard; to perform satisfactorily.
The new candidate cuts the mustard in terms of qualifications and experience. Our current infrastructure doesn't cut the mustard for the growing demands of the business.
to put all eggs in one basket
/pʊt ɔːl eɡz ɪn wʌn ˈbɑː.skɪt/
To invest all resources, hopes, or effort into a single plan or option, risking everything on one outcome.
The company shouldn't put all eggs in one basket by relying solely on one major client. Investors are hesitant to put all their eggs in one basket with a single stock.
meticulous
/məˈtɪk.jə.ləs/
Very careful and precise; paying close attention to every detail.
Her meticulous approach to project management ensures nothing is overlooked. The financial report was prepared with meticulous attention to accuracy.
a nitpicker
/ə ˈnɪt.pɪk.ər/
A person who focuses on small, unimportant details or faults rather than the bigger picture; someone who criticizes minor imperfections.
He's a bit of a nitpicker when it comes to formatting, but his attention to detail is valuable. Even a nitpicker would find little to fault with this proposal.
