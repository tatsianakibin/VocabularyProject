UNIT 1 - First Impressions

ineffective (negative)
/ˌɪn.ɪˈfek.tɪv/
Not producing the desired result or effect; failing to achieve the intended purpose.
An ineffective method can waste time and resources. The company's ineffective strategy led to a loss of market share.
arrogant (negative)
/ˈær.ə.ɡənt/
Having an exaggerated sense of one's own importance or abilities; displaying excessive pride or self-importance.
His arrogant attitude made it difficult to work with him. The arrogant behavior of the CEO alienated the team.
functional (neutral/positive)
/ˈfʌŋk.ʃən.əl/
Designed to be practical and useful rather than attractive; serving a purpose effectively.
The functional design of the office maximizes productivity. A functional approach to problem-solving is often more effective than a creative one.
modest (positive)
/ˈmɒd.ɪst/
Not large or expensive; humble and moderate in size, scope, or ambition.
She works from a modest office. The company made a modest proposal for the merger. He drives a modest phone and uses a modest car.
ostentatious (negative)
/ˌɒs.tenˈteɪ.ʃəs/
Designed to impress or attract notice; excessively showy and meant to display wealth or importance.
The ostentatious display of wealth made others uncomfortable. His ostentatious lifestyle and over-the-top car reflected his insecurity.
trustworthy (positive)
/ˈtrʌst.wɜː.ðɪ/
Able to be relied on as honest or truthful; deserving of confidence and trust.
A trustworthy source is essential for accurate research. She is a trustworthy person who always delivers on her commitments.
suspicious (negative)
/səˈspɪʃ.əs/
Having or showing a feeling that something is wrong or someone is behaving badly; distrustful.
Suspicious behavior can undermine trust in the workplace. The circumstances surrounding the deal were highly suspicious. A suspicious-looking van was parked outside. I am suspicious about his motives.
complex (neutral)
/ˈkɒm.pleks/
Consisting of many different and connected parts; complicated and difficult to understand or deal with.
Solving a complex problem requires careful analysis. A complex system with multiple variables needs thorough testing before implementation.
wary (neutral/negative)
/ˈweə.ri/
Cautious and careful; alert to possible danger or problems; showing caution or suspicion.
Be wary of strangers offering unsolicited advice. Employees remain wary of the new management system. Many parents are wary about standardized tests.
favourable (positive)
/ˈfeɪ.vər.ə.bəl/
Expressing approval or agreement; advantageous and likely to lead to success or a positive outcome.
The interview made a favourable impression on the hiring committee. Favourable conditions in the market made it an ideal time to launch the product.
monotonous (negative)
/məˈnɒt.ən.əs/
Lacking variety or interest; tedious and boring because of being unchanging or repetitive.
The monotonous tone of voice made the presentation difficult to follow. Working on monotonous tasks all day can lead to decreased productivity and employee dissatisfaction.

UNIT 3 - On Schedule

upfront planning
/ˈʌpfrʌnt ˈplænɪŋ/
Planning done at the beginning, before starting work; preparation and organization completed before a project begins.
Good upfront planning helps avoid problems later in the project. Do upfront planning to ensure the project runs smoothly.
budget constraints
/ˈbʌdʒɪt kənˈstreɪnts/
Limitations on the money available to spend; restrictions on financial resources.
We had to simplify the design due to budget constraints. Work within budget constraints to ensure the project remains financially viable.
accurate forecast
/ˈækjərət ˈfɔːrkæst/
A prediction that is correct or very close to reality; an estimate of future costs, timeline, or resources that is precise.
An accurate forecast helps managers allocate resources properly. Make an accurate forecast before starting the project.
realistic budget
/ˌriːəˈlɪstɪk ˈbʌdʒɪt/
A financial plan based on what is actually possible; a budget that accurately reflects real costs and circumstances.
We need to create a realistic budget that accounts for unexpected costs and market changes.
miss the deadline
/mɪs ðə ˈded.laɪn/
Fail to complete something by the required time; not finish work by the agreed-upon date.
If we miss the deadline, we'll lose the client and damage our reputation.
run into problems
/rʌn ˈɪn.tə ˈprɒb.ləmz/
Face unexpected difficulties; encounter issues or obstacles during a project.
We ran into problems when the supplier delayed the shipment. The team will run into problems if resources are not allocated properly.
stay on track
/steɪ ɒn træk/
Continue making progress according to plan; maintain the planned schedule and timeline.
Daily meetings help the team stay on track with their goals. We need to stay on track to meet the project deadline.
go over the budget
/ɡəʊ ˈəʊ.vər ðə ˈbʌdʒɪt/
Spend more money than planned; exceed the allocated financial resources.
The construction project went over the budget by 20%. If we go over the budget, we'll need to cut expenses elsewhere.
fall behind schedule
/fɔːl bɪˈhaɪnd ˈʃedjuːl/
Become late compared to the planned timeline; progress slower than expected.
Bad weather caused us to fall behind schedule. We cannot fall behind schedule or we'll miss the launch date.
make the launch date
/meɪk ðə ˈlɔːntʃ deɪt/
Successfully complete work by the planned release time; meet the scheduled deadline for launching a product or project.
The team worked overtime to make the launch date. We must make the launch date to stay competitive in the market.
