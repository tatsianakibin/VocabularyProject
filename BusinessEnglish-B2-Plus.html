UNIT 1 - Market Research & Product Launch

endorsement
/ɪnˈdɔːs.mənt/
The act of saying that you approve of or support something or someone, especially publicly.
Celebrity endorsement is a popular way of promoting products.
to conduct online surveys
/kənˈdʌkt ˈɒn.laɪn ˈsɜː.veɪz/
To carry out or organize questionnaires distributed through the internet to collect data from respondents.
The marketing team decided to conduct online surveys to understand customer preferences before the product launch.
to create a focus group to gather opinions
/kriˈeɪt ə ˈfəʊ.kəs ɡruːp tə ˈɡæð.ər əˈpɪn.jənz/
To assemble a small, diverse group of people to discuss and provide feedback on a specific topic, product, or service.
We need to create a focus group to gather opinions on the new packaging design.
customer satisfaction index/rating
/ˈkʌs.tə.mər ˌsæt.ɪsˈfæk.ʃən ˈɪn.deks / ˈreɪ.tɪŋ/
A measurement or score that indicates how satisfied customers are with a company's products or services.
Our customer satisfaction rating has improved by 15% since we introduced the new support system.
in-depth interviews
/ˌɪnˈdepθ ˈɪn.tə.vjuːz/
Detailed, thorough conversations with individuals to gain comprehensive insights into their opinions, experiences, or behaviors.
The researchers conducted in-depth interviews with twenty business owners to understand their challenges.
desk research
/desk rɪˈsɜːtʃ/
Research conducted by analyzing existing information and data from published sources rather than collecting new data directly.
Before investing in primary research, we completed extensive desk research using industry reports and competitor analysis.
to launch a product
UK: /lɔːntʃ ə ˈprɒd.ʌkt/ US: /lɑːntʃ ə ˈprɑː.dʌkt/
To introduce a new product to the market, making it available for purchase by consumers.
The company plans to launch a product next quarter that will revolutionize the smartphone market.
a target audience
/ə ˈtɑː.ɡɪt ˈɔː.di.əns/
The specific group of consumers most likely to want or need your product or service, at whom marketing efforts are directed.
Our target audience for this luxury watch brand is professionals aged 30-50 with high disposable income.
sample size
/ˈsɑːm.pəl saɪz/
The number of participants or observations included in a research study or survey.
The researchers used a sample size of 100 participants for their study.
to conduct quantitative/qualitative analysis
/kənˈdʌkt ˈkwɒn.tɪ.tə.tɪv / ˈkwɒl.ɪ.tə.tɪv əˈnæl.ə.sɪs/
To perform systematic examination of numerical data (quantitative) or non-numerical data such as opinions and experiences (qualitative).
After collecting survey responses, we will conduct quantitative analysis to identify trends and conduct qualitative analysis to understand customer motivations.
a product tester
/ə ˈprɒd.ʌkt ˈtes.tər/
A person who evaluates and tests products before they are released to ensure quality, functionality, and user satisfaction.
We recruited fifty product testers to trial the new software and provide detailed feedback.
a researcher on...
/ə rɪˈsɜː.tʃər ɒn/
A person who conducts systematic investigation and study in a particular field or subject area.
She is a leading researcher on consumer behavior and has published numerous articles on the subject.
to question respondents
/tə ˈkwes.tʃən rɪˈspɒn.dənts/
To ask people who are participating in a survey or study specific questions to gather their responses and opinions.
During the focus group session, we questioned respondents about their shopping habits and brand preferences.
to have an impact on...
/həv ən ˈɪm.pækt ɒn/
To have a noticeable effect or influence on something or someone.
Social media campaigns can have a significant impact on brand awareness and customer engagement.
to gauge the mood/the reaction
/ɡeɪdʒ ðə muːd / ðə riˈæk.ʃən/
To measure, estimate, or assess the general feeling or response of people toward something.
The CEO held a town hall meeting to gauge the mood of employees regarding the proposed organizational changes.
to gather information
/ˈɡæð.ər ˌɪn.fəˈmeɪ.ʃən/
To collect data, facts, or details from various sources for a specific purpose.
We need to gather information from all departments before making a final decision on the budget allocation.
to be a viable proposition
/biː ə ˈvaɪ.ə.bəl ˌprɒp.əˈzɪʃ.ən/
To be a practical, feasible, and potentially successful idea, plan, or business opportunity.
After reviewing the market analysis, the board concluded that expanding into Southeast Asia would be a viable proposition.
to be based on data analysis
/biː beɪst ɒn ˈdeɪ.tə əˈnæl.ə.sɪs/
To have decisions, conclusions, or strategies founded on the systematic examination of data and information.
Our marketing strategy is based on data analysis that shows millennials prefer video content over written articles.
primary research / secondary research
/ˈpraɪ.mər.i rɪˈsɜːtʃ / ˈsek.ən.dri rɪˈsɜːtʃ/
Primary research is original data collected directly from sources through methods like surveys or interviews. Secondary research uses existing data collected by others.
We conducted primary research through customer interviews and supplemented it with secondary research from industry publications.
a techie
/ˈtek.i/
An informal term for a person who is very knowledgeable about or enthusiastic about technology.
As a real techie, John can fix any computer problem and is always the first to try new gadgets.
tech-savvy
/ˈtek ˌsæv.i/
Having a good knowledge and understanding of modern technology, especially computers and digital devices.
We're looking for tech-savvy employees who can adapt quickly to our new software systems.
on a daily basis
/ɒn ə ˈdeɪ.li ˈbeɪ.sɪs/
Every day; as a regular daily occurrence or routine.
Our customer service team handles over 500 inquiries on a daily basis.
to entice
/ɪnˈtaɪs/
To attract or tempt someone by offering something appealing or desirable.
The company offered a generous bonus package to entice top talent from competing firms.
go for it
/ɡəʊ fər ɪt/
An informal expression meaning to take action or pursue an opportunity with determination and confidence.
If you think this business idea has potential, I say go for it and don't let fear hold you back.
tread lightly
/tred ˈlaɪt.li/
To proceed carefully and cautiously, especially in a sensitive or potentially problematic situation.
When discussing salary cuts with the team, the manager knew she needed to tread lightly to avoid causing panic.
tailor something to someone
/ˈteɪ.lər ˈsʌm.θɪŋ tə ˈsʌm.wʌn/
To customize or adapt something specifically to meet the needs, preferences, or requirements of a particular person or group.
We tailor our training programs to each client's specific industry needs and organizational culture.

UNIT 2 - Workplace Relationships & Collaboration

cooperative atmosphere
/kəʊˈɒp.ər.ə.tɪv ˈæt.mə.sfɪər/
An environment where people work together willingly and helpfully toward common goals.
The manager fostered a cooperative atmosphere by encouraging team members to share ideas and support each other's projects.
mutually beneficial
/ˈmjuː.tʃu.ə.li ˌben.ɪˈfɪʃ.əl/
Advantageous or helpful to all parties involved in a relationship or agreement.
The partnership between our companies has proven to be mutually beneficial, with both sides increasing their market share.
to call the shots
/kɔːl ðə ʃɒts/
To make the important decisions and have control or authority over a situation.
As the project manager, Sarah calls the shots on all major decisions regarding budget allocation and timelines.
to have the best interests of someone at heart / to have someone's best interests at heart
/həv ðə best ˈɪn.trəsts əv ˈsʌm.wʌn ət hɑːt/
To genuinely care about someone's wellbeing and to act in ways that benefit them.
Despite the difficult feedback, I know my mentor has my best interests at heart and wants me to succeed.
to be a two-way street
/biː ə tuː weɪ striːt/
A situation involving mutual responsibility, effort, or benefit; something that requires give-and-take from both parties.
Trust in the workplace is a two-way street – employees need to trust management, but management must also trust their teams.
to be interested in inclusion
/biː ˈɪn.trə.stɪd ɪn ɪnˈkluː.ʒən/
To care about ensuring that all people are welcomed, valued, and able to participate fully regardless of their backgrounds or differences.
Companies that are genuinely interested in inclusion actively recruit diverse candidates and create policies that support all employees.
to foster a nurturing environment
/ˈfɒs.tər ə ˈnɜː.tʃər.ɪŋ ɪnˈvaɪ.rən.mənt/
To promote and develop a supportive atmosphere where people can grow, develop, and feel cared for.
The best leaders foster a nurturing environment where employees feel safe to take risks and learn from mistakes.
to feel isolated
/fiːl ˈaɪ.sə.leɪ.tɪd/
To experience loneliness or disconnection from others; to feel separated or excluded from a group.
Remote workers sometimes feel isolated from their colleagues and miss the social interaction of office life.
to grow mistrust
/ɡrəʊ mɪsˈtrʌst/
To develop or increase a lack of confidence or faith in someone or something over time.
When management repeatedly broke their promises, employees began to grow mistrust toward company announcements.
to be candid
/biː ˈkæn.dɪd/
To be honest, open, and straightforward in communication; to express thoughts truthfully without hiding anything.
During the performance review, the supervisor was candid about both the employee's strengths and areas needing improvement.
peer pressure
/pɪər ˈpreʃ.ər/
The influence or pressure from people of the same age, status, or group to behave or act in a certain way.
Some employees work excessive overtime not because it's required, but due to peer pressure from colleagues who stay late every day.
a corporate event
/ə ˈkɔː.pər.ət ɪˈvent/
An organized gathering or activity sponsored by a company, typically for employees, clients, or stakeholders, such as conferences, team-building activities, or celebrations.
The annual corporate event included team-building workshops, a gala dinner, and awards ceremony to recognize outstanding employees.
kill someone with kindness
/kɪl ˈsʌmwʌn wɪð ˈkaɪndnəs/
To respond to someone's hostility or negativity by being extremely nice or good to them.
When dealing with difficult clients, our strategy is to kill them with kindness and provide exceptional service.
make a hard call
/meɪk ə hɑːd kɔːl/
To make a difficult or challenging decision, especially one that might have significant consequences.
As a manager, sometimes you have to make hard calls about staffing that affect people's lives.
a cautionary tale
/ə ˈkɔːʃənəri teɪl/
A story or example that serves as a warning about the possible consequences of certain actions or behaviors.
The failed merger became a cautionary tale about the importance of proper due diligence.
can't cut it
/kɑːnt kʌt ɪt/
To be unable to meet the required standards or handle the demands of a situation (informal).
After three months, it became clear that the new hire couldn't cut it in such a fast-paced environment.
to exude
/ɪɡˈzjuːd/
To display or show a quality or feeling very strongly and naturally.
The new sales director exudes confidence in every presentation she gives.
keep someone on even
/kiːp ˈsʌmwʌn ɒn ˈiːvən/
To maintain stability or keep someone or something in a steady, balanced state.
A good manager knows how to keep their team on even during times of change and uncertainty.
clumsiness
/ˈklʌm.zi.nəs/
The quality of being awkward in movement or handling things, lacking grace or physical coordination.
His clumsiness during the product demonstration made the potential clients uncomfortable and affected the sales pitch.
to sugarcoat
/ˈʃʊɡ.ə.kəʊt/
To make something unpleasant seem more attractive or acceptable by presenting it in a more pleasant way.
The manager decided not to sugarcoat the company's financial difficulties when addressing the team.
to streamline something
/ˈstriːm.laɪn ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To make a system, organization, or process more effective and efficient by employing faster or simpler working methods.
The company hired consultants to help streamline their manufacturing process and reduce costs.
a fine line
/ə faɪn laɪn/
A very subtle or slight difference between two things that are otherwise very similar; a delicate balance between two states or situations.
There's a fine line between being a supportive manager and micromanaging your team's work.
devise
/dɪˈvaɪz/
To plan, invent, or create something new or a way of doing something through careful thought.
The team needs to devise a strategy to improve customer retention rates.
come up with
/kʌm ʌp wɪð/
To think of or produce an idea, suggestion, or solution.
We need to come up with innovative solutions to address these customer complaints.
get carried away with
/ɡet ˈkærid əˈweɪ wɪð/
To become overly excited or enthusiastic about something, losing self-control or a sense of proportion.
Don't get carried away with the new project ideas before we've secured the budget.
to be worlds apart
/biː wɜːldz əˈpɑːt/
To be very different from each other in beliefs, values, opinions, or positions; to have little in common.
The two departments were worlds apart in their approach to customer service, causing collaboration challenges.
a catalyst for change
/ə ˈkæt.ə.lɪst fər tʃeɪndʒ/
A person, event, or situation that precipitates or triggers significant transformation or change.
The new leadership proved to be a catalyst for change, introducing innovative processes and improving company culture.
to devise a robust strategy
/dɪˈvaɪz ə rəʊˈbʌst ˈstræt.ə.dʒi/
To create or develop a strong, well-built plan that is resilient and able to withstand challenges.
The management team worked together to devise a robust strategy that would help the company remain competitive during economic uncertainty.
to sing from the same hymn sheet
/sɪŋ frɒm ðə seɪm hɪm ʃiːt/
To be in agreement; to express consistent messages or adopt the same stance on an issue; to be unified in approach.
For the presentation to be effective, all team members need to sing from the same hymn sheet regarding the company's strategic goals.
a foregone conclusion
/ə ˈfɔːɡɒn kənˈkluː.ʒən/
A result or outcome that is certain or inevitable; a decision that appears to have been made beforehand.
Although they conducted an election, many employees felt the promotion decision was a foregone conclusion.
to align perspectives
/əˈlaɪn pərˈspek.tɪvz/
To bring different viewpoints or opinions into agreement or harmony; to ensure shared understanding across a group.
The project requires us to align perspectives between the sales and product teams to ensure we're working toward the same goals.
a ballpark figure
/ə ˈbɔːlpɑːk ˈfɪɡ.ər/
An approximate or rough estimate of a number or amount; a general idea of size or cost without exact precision.
Can you give me a ballpark figure for the project budget so we can begin preliminary discussions?
to lag behind the curve
/læɡ bɪˈhaɪnd ðə kɜːv/
To fall behind current trends, developments, or standards; to be slower in adopting new practices or technologies than competitors.
If we don't invest in AI technology soon, our company will lag behind the curve and lose market share to more innovative competitors.
to look forward to
/tə lʊk ˈfɔːr.wərd tuː/
To anticipate or expect something with pleasure or optimism; to be excited about something that will happen in the future.
We look forward to collaborating with your team on this exciting new project.
to observe tangible results
/əbˈzɜːv ˈtæn.dʒə.bəl rɪˈzʌlts/
To see or notice concrete, measurable outcomes that can be clearly identified or verified.
After implementing the new training program, the company was able to observe tangible results in employee productivity within three months.
to lay the foundation for
/tə leɪ ðə ˈfaʊn.deɪ.ʃən fɔː/
To establish the basic framework or groundwork that enables something to develop or succeed in the future.
The strategic partnerships we formed this year will lay the foundation for significant growth in the coming years.
in line with
/ɪn laɪn wɪð/
In accordance with; in agreement with; consistent with something.
The company's new sustainability initiatives are in line with our commitment to environmental responsibility.
performance benchmarks
/pəˈfɔːr.məns ˈbenʃ.mɑːrks/
Measurable standards or reference points used to evaluate and compare the effectiveness of performance or achievement.
The team established clear performance benchmarks at the beginning of the project to track progress and measure success.

UNIT 3 - Digital Finance & Innovation

to take over as ...
/teɪk ˈəʊ.vər əz/
To assume a position or role and become responsible for a job or duty previously held by someone else.
She will take over as CEO next month, succeeding the current leadership.
it's not a stretch to imagine that
/ɪts nɒt ə stretʃ tə ɪˈmæd͡ʒ.ɪn ðæt/
It is not difficult or unreasonable to understand or believe that something could happen; to conceive of something as plausible.
It's not a stretch to imagine that cryptocurrency will become more mainstream in the coming years.
be averse to ...
/biː əˈvɜːs tə/
To be strongly disinclined or opposed to something; to have a strong feeling against something.
Many traditional bankers are averse to embracing new blockchain technologies.
be digitally literate
/biː ˈdɪdʒ.ɪ.təl.i ˈlɪt.ər.ət/
To have the knowledge and skills to use digital technology effectively and confidently.
Employees need to be digitally literate to work with modern financial platforms and software.
becoming ubiquitous
/bɪˈkʌm.ɪŋ juːˈbɪk.wɪ.təs/
Becoming present everywhere at the same time; becoming widely distributed or commonplace.
Mobile payment systems are becoming ubiquitous in retail environments across the globe.
bring traceability and transparency
/brɪŋ ˌtreɪ.sə.ˈbɪl.ə.ti ənd træns.ˈpær.ən.si/
To provide the ability to track and verify something, and to make information clear and visible to all parties involved.
Blockchain technology can bring traceability and transparency to supply chain management and financial transactions.
to recoup
/rɪˈkuːp/
To recover or retrieve something, especially money that was spent or lost; to get back an amount equal to a cost.
The company invested heavily in digital transformation and hopes to recoup their investment within three years.
unbanked people
/ʌnˈbæŋkt ˈpiː.pəl/
People who do not have access to or do not use traditional banking services, such as bank accounts or loans.
Fintech companies aim to provide financial services to unbanked people in developing countries.
a finite, physical sum of money
/ə ˈfaɪ.naɪt ˈfɪz.ɪ.kəl sʌm əv ˈmʌn.i/
A limited amount of tangible currency or cash that physically exists, as opposed to digital money or unlimited credit.
Bitcoin operates on the principle of a finite, physical sum of money, with a maximum supply capped at 21 million coins.
moral
/ˈmɒr.əl/
Relating to principles of right and wrong behavior; concerning the principles or rules of good conduct.
The company faces a moral obligation to ensure its practices are ethical and environmentally responsible.
morale
/məˈrɑːl/
The confidence, enthusiasm, and sense of purpose that a person or group has; the general feeling of well-being or satisfaction.
Employee morale has improved significantly since the company announced the new benefits package.
to draw up a financial plan
/drɔː ʌp ə faɪˈnæn.ʃəl plæn/
To create or prepare a detailed written plan that outlines financial goals, budgets, and strategies for managing money.
Before launching the new product, the finance team needs to draw up a financial plan that covers all projected costs and revenues.
to weigh the financial risks and rewards
/weɪ ðə faɪˈnæn.ʃəl rɪsks ənd rɪˈwɔːdz/
To carefully consider and compare the potential dangers or losses against the potential benefits or gains of a financial decision.
Before investing in the startup, the board decided to weigh the financial risks and rewards over a three-year period.
to incur unexpected costs
/ɪnˈkɜː ʌnɪkˈspek.tɪd kɒsts/
To experience or become responsible for expenses that were not anticipated or planned for.
During the construction project, the company incurred unexpected costs when they discovered structural problems with the building.
to navigate local bureaucracy
/ˈnæv.ɪ.ɡeɪt ˈləʊ.kəl ˌbjuːrəʊˈkræt.ɪ.si/
To skillfully handle and work through the complex system of regulations, procedures, and government departments in a particular area.
Expanding into a new country requires learning how to navigate local bureaucracy and understand tax regulations.
to be lured by the low price
/biː ˈljʊəd baɪ ðə ləʊ praɪs/
To be attracted or enticed by something that costs less money, often without considering potential hidden problems or inferior quality.
Investors should not be lured by the low price of a property without conducting proper inspections and due diligence.
to assess the return on investment
/əˈses ðə rɪˈtɜːn ɒn ɪnˈves.tmənt/
To evaluate or measure the profit or financial gain generated from an investment relative to the amount of money invested.
The finance department will assess the return on investment for each project to determine which ones are most profitable.
to be financially viable
/biː faɪˈnæn.ʃəl.i ˈvaɪ.ə.bəl/
To be able to succeed economically; to have a reasonable chance of generating sufficient income to sustain itself.
The startup needed to prove to investors that its business model would be financially viable before securing funding.
to allocate a renovation budget
/ˈæl.ə.keɪt ə ˌren.əˈveɪ.ʃən ˈbʌdʒ.ɪt/
To set aside and distribute a specific amount of money for repairing, updating, or improving a property or building.
The property management company decided to allocate a renovation budget of 500,000 euros for modernizing the office building.
to restore a property to livable condition
/rɪˈstɔː ə ˈprɒp.ə.ti tə ˈlɪv.ə.bəl kənˈdɪʃ.ən/
To repair and renovate a building or home so that it is safe, comfortable, and suitable for people to live in.
The investors purchased the abandoned house with plans to restore it to livable condition and then resell it for a profit.
stocks
/stɒks/
Shares or ownership portions of a company that can be bought and sold; equity investments that represent a claim on the company's assets and earnings.
The retirement fund is diversified with a mix of stocks, bonds, and real estate investments.
bonds
/bɒndz/
Fixed-income securities that represent a loan made by an investor to a government or company; the issuer promises to pay back the principal plus interest.
Government bonds are generally considered safer investments than stocks because they offer guaranteed returns.
to shell out for / to fork out for something
/tə ʃel aʊt fɔː / tə fɔːk aʊt fɔː ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To spend a large amount of money on something, often reluctantly or unexpectedly.
We had to shell out for a lot of money for the new equipment. She didn't want to fork out for that expensive software.
to splash out on something
/tə splæʃ aʊt ɒn ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To spend a large sum of money on something luxurious or special; to treat yourself to something expensive.
After getting her promotion, she decided to splash out on a luxury holiday to celebrate.
to run up huge debts
/tə rʌn ʌp ˈhjuːdʒ dets/
To accumulate large amounts of money owed; to incur significant debt over time.
The young entrepreneur ran up huge debts trying to launch his startup before it became profitable.
to set up a dedicated savings fund
/tə set ʌp ə ˈded.ɪ.keɪ.tɪd ˈseɪv.ɪŋz fʌnd/
To establish a separate financial account or reserve set aside for a specific savings goal or purpose.
The company decided to set up a dedicated savings fund for employee retirement and future investments.
to take out a mortgage/loan
/tə teɪk aʊt ə ˈmɔː.ɡɪdʒ / ləʊn/
To borrow a large sum of money from a bank or financial institution, often for purchasing a property or major asset.
First-time homebuyers often need to take out a mortgage to afford a house. We took out a loan to expand our business.
to build a nest egg
/tə bɪld ə nest eɡ/
To save and accumulate money over time, especially for future security or a major life goal like retirement.
By investing regularly in her pension, she has managed to build a nest egg for her retirement years.
financial backing
/faɪˈnæn.ʃəl ˈbæk.ɪŋ/
Financial support or funding provided by investors, banks, or sponsors to help a person or business succeed.
The startup secured financial backing from venture capitalists to develop their innovative technology platform.

UNIT 4 - Business Growth & Market Strategy

to be unequivocal
/biː ʌnɪˈkwɪv.ə.kəl/
To be absolutely clear, certain, and leaving no doubt; to express something in a definite and unmistakable way.
The CEO was unequivocal about the company's commitment to environmental sustainability in her annual address.
to clamp down on something
/klæmp daʊn ɒn ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To take firm and strict action to stop or control something, especially something illegal or undesirable.
The government has decided to clamp down on tax evasion by increasing inspections and penalties.
spending habits
/ˈspen.dɪŋ ˈhæb.ɪts/
The usual patterns and behaviors people or organizations demonstrate when they spend money, including frequency, amount, and types of purchases.
Understanding customer spending habits helps retailers design more effective marketing campaigns and product placement strategies.
the big picture
/ðə bɪɡ ˈpɪk.tʃər/
The overall situation, context, or general view of something, considering all important aspects rather than just small details.
While it's important to focus on quarterly results, we must never lose sight of the big picture and our long-term strategic goals.
shrinking market
/ˈʃrɪŋ.kɪŋ ˈmɑː.kɪt/
An industry or market segment that is experiencing declining sales, fewer customers, or reduced overall size and opportunity.
The company recognized that the shrinking market for physical media required diversification into digital streaming services.
to be dependent on
/biː dɪˈpen.dənt ɒn/
To rely on or need something or someone; to be unable to function or succeed without something.
The organization's success is heavily dependent on securing government contracts, which creates vulnerability to policy changes.
collective targets
/kəˈlek.tɪv ˈtɑː.ɡɪts/
Goals or objectives set for a group, team, or entire organization that all members work together to achieve.
The sales team established collective targets of £2 million for the quarter and implemented a bonus structure to incentivize collaboration.
to redistribute
/ˌriː.dɪˈstrɪb.juːt/
To distribute something again or differently; to allocate or assign resources, money, or responsibilities in a new way.
The manager decided to redistribute the project workload to ensure fairer distribution of responsibilities across the team.
to be persistent
/biː pərˈsɪs.tənt/
To continue firmly and steadily despite difficulties, opposition, or setbacks; to persevere without giving up.
Successful entrepreneurs are persistent in pursuing their goals even when facing rejection and failure.

UNIT 5 - Environmental Challenges

fall short of
/fɔːl ʃɔːrt əv/
To fail to reach or achieve something; to not meet an expected standard or goal.
Many countries fall short of their carbon emission reduction targets due to industrial activity and transportation.
carbon offset
/ˈkɑː.bən ˈɒf.set/
A reduction in greenhouse gases made to compensate for emissions produced elsewhere; an investment in environmental projects to neutralize carbon footprint.
Companies purchase carbon offsets through reforestation projects to compensate for their operational emissions.
shrinking habitats
/ˈʃrɪŋ.kɪŋ ˈhæb.ɪ.tæts/
Natural environments that are becoming smaller in area, reducing the available space for plants and animals to live.
Shrinking habitats in the Amazon rainforest threaten the survival of numerous species.
biodiversity
/ˌbaɪ.oʊˈdɪ.vɜː.sə.ti/
The variety of different plants, animals, and microorganisms in a particular environment or on Earth.
Protecting biodiversity is crucial for maintaining ecosystem balance and ensuring food security for future generations.
endangered species
/ɪnˈdeɪn.dʒərd ˈspiː.ʃiːz/
Plants or animals that are in danger of extinction or disappearing from the planet.
Conservation efforts are focused on protecting endangered species such as giant pandas and African elephants.
carbon emissions
/ˈkɑː.bən ɪˈmɪ.ʃənz/
The release of carbon dioxide and other carbon compounds into the atmosphere, primarily from burning fossil fuels.
Reducing carbon emissions is essential to combating climate change and meeting international environmental agreements.
fossil fuels
/ˈfɒs.əl ˈfjuː.əlz/
Energy sources formed from ancient organic matter, such as coal, oil, and natural gas, that are non-renewable and produce carbon emissions when burned.
The transition away from fossil fuels toward renewable energy is critical for sustainable development.
exerts severe pressure on
/ɪɡˈzɜːts sɪˈvɪər ˈpreʃ.ər ɒn/
To put intense strain, stress, or demanding force on something or someone; to create difficult conditions.
Climate change exerts severe pressure on agricultural systems and fresh water supplies worldwide.
finite resources
/ˈfaɪ.naɪt ˈrez.ɔː.sɪz/
Materials or supplies that have a limited quantity and cannot be replaced or regenerated at the rate they are consumed.
Oil and minerals are finite resources that we must use wisely to ensure availability for future generations.
deforestation
/ˌdeɪ.fɒr.ɪsˈteɪ.ʃən/
The large-scale removal of forests or trees, typically to make way for agriculture, urban development, or logging.
Deforestation in tropical regions is a major contributor to biodiversity loss and climate change.
demographic projections
/ˌdem.əˈɡræf.ɪk prəˈdʒek.ʃənz/
Forecasts or predictions about future population changes, including growth, age distribution, and geographic distribution.
Demographic projections indicate that global population will reach 10 billion by 2050, increasing pressure on environmental resources.
depleting resources
/dɪˈpliː.tɪŋ ˈrez.ɔː.sɪz/
Using up or exhausting natural resources at a rate faster than they can be replenished or regenerated.
Overfishing is rapidly depleting resources in our oceans and threatening marine ecosystems.
waste disposal
/weɪst dɪˈspəʊ.zəl/
The process of getting rid of trash, garbage, and other unwanted materials in a safe and environmentally responsible manner.
Proper waste disposal and recycling programs are essential for reducing landfill pollution.
to contaminate
/kənˈtæm.ɪ.neɪt/
To make something unclean or impure by adding harmful or unwanted substances; to pollute.
Industrial factories can contaminate groundwater with toxic chemicals, making it unsafe for drinking.
precious commodity
/ˈpreʃ.əs kəˈmɒd.ə.ti/
A valuable good or resource that is highly desired, rare, or essential; something of great worth or importance.
In many arid regions, clean water has become a precious commodity as droughts intensify.
pristine environments
/prɪˈstiːn ɪnˈvaɪ.rən.mənts/
Natural areas that are untouched, unspoiled, and in their original, pure condition; wilderness areas free from human pollution or degradation.
Conservation organizations work to protect pristine environments such as coral reefs and rainforests from development and exploitation.
to come to a standstill
/kʌm tə ə ˈstænd.stɪl/
To stop moving or functioning; to come to a complete halt or cessation of progress.
Production came to a standstill due to the power outage. Traffic came to a standstill during the rush hour congestion.
to extort money from someone
/ɪkˈstɔːt ˈmʌn.i frəm ˈsʌm.wʌn/
To obtain money or something of value from someone through coercion, threats, or blackmail.
Cybercriminals often extort money from companies by threatening to release sensitive data. The gang attempted to extort money from the business owner.
software incompatibility
/ˈsɒf.weər ɪn.kəm.pæt.ɪˈbɪl.ə.ti/
The lack of ability for different software systems or programs to work together properly; when software cannot interact or function correctly with other systems.
The company faced software incompatibility issues when trying to integrate the new system with legacy applications. Software incompatibility delays the project implementation.
to be infected with ransomware
/biː ɪnˈfek.tɪd wɪð ˈræn.səm.weər/
To have one's computer, network, or system compromised by malicious software that encrypts files and demands payment for their release.
The hospital's systems were infected with ransomware, forcing staff to revert to manual procedures. Businesses need robust security to avoid being infected with ransomware.
uproar
/ˈʌp.rɔːr/
A situation in which a lot of people complain about something angrily or make a lot of noise; public outrage.
The new tax policy caused an uproar among the citizens.
a lectern
/ˈlek.tən/
A stand with a sloped top for holding books or papers, used when reading aloud or making a speech.
The professor placed her notes on the lectern before beginning the lecture.
to hurl
/hɜːl/
To throw something with great force, usually in an angry or violent way.
The angry protestor hurled a stone at the building.
unwavering
/ʌnˈweɪ.vər.ɪŋ/
Never changing or becoming weaker; firm and determined; showing constant support or belief.
She showed unwavering support for her friend during the difficult time.
vertiginous
/vəˈtɪdʒ.ɪ.nəs/
Causing dizziness, especially as a result of being very high up; extremely high, steep, or rapid in rate of change.
The vertiginous height of the skyscraper made her feel dizzy. The company experienced vertiginous growth after launching the new product.
full-blown
/ˌfʊlˈbləʊn/
Having all the characteristics of something; complete and fully developed; at its most serious stage.
What started as a small disagreement became a full-blown argument. The company faces a full-blown crisis if immediate action is not taken.
impartially
/ɪmˈpɑː.ʃəl.i/
In a way that does not support any of the sides involved in an argument; fairly and without bias.
The judge listened to both sides impartially before making a decision. Managers must judge employee performance impartially to maintain trust and fairness.
procurement
/prəˈkjʊə.mənt/
The process of getting supplies or services, especially for a government or organization; the act of acquiring or obtaining something.
The company's procurement department is responsible for purchasing office supplies. Government procurement procedures are strictly regulated to ensure transparency and fairness.
to exploit
/ɪkˈsplɔɪt/
To make use of something or someone in a way that helps you achieve your goals; to utilize fully or effectively.
The team learned to exploit their colleagues' expertise to improve project outcomes. Successful entrepreneurs exploit every available opportunity and resource.
to deploy
/dɪˈplɔɪ/
To put something into use or action; to position or arrange resources strategically for effective use.
The company will deploy the new app next month across all platforms. We need to deploy additional resources to meet the growing demand.
to roll (something) out
/rəʊl aʊt/
To make a new product, service, or system available for the first time; to introduce something gradually to a wider audience.
The tech company will roll out its new operating system to users worldwide next quarter. We are excited to roll out our redesigned website to all customers.
tirelessly
/ˈtaɪə.ləs.li/
In an energetic and continuous way; without becoming tired or losing enthusiasm; persistently and dedicatedly.
The project manager worked tirelessly to meet the deadline. The team cleaned the office tirelessly, ensuring every detail was perfect.
to insult
/ɪnˈsʌlt/
To say or do something to someone that is rude or offensive; to treat someone disrespectfully.
It is unprofessional to insult a colleague during a business meeting. The manager's dismissive comments felt like an insult to the team's hard work.
a thirst for knowledge
/ə θɜːst fər ˈnɒl.ɪdʒ/
A strong desire to learn and understand new information; an enthusiastic drive to acquire knowledge and skills.
She has a thirst for knowledge, which is why she reads so many books every month. Successful professionals maintain a constant thirst for knowledge to stay competitive in their fields.

UNIT 6 - Business Meeting Phrases

Can you take a look?
/kæn juː teɪk ə lʊk/
A polite request asking someone to review or examine something; to ask someone to check or evaluate something for you.
Can you take a look at the proposal and let me know if you see any issues? I'd appreciate it if you could take a look at these figures.
It's a bit tight.
/ɪts ə bɪt taɪt/
A situation where there is limited time, resources, or space; when conditions are constrained or difficult.
The budget for this project is a bit tight, so we need to find cost-saving solutions. The timeline is a bit tight, but I think we can manage it.
Let's catch up later.
/lets kætʃ ʌp ˈleɪ.tər/
A suggestion to meet or talk at a future time to discuss something; to schedule a later conversation.
I'm busy right now, but let's catch up later this afternoon. Let's catch up later when we both have more time to talk properly.
We're getting some pushback.
/wɪər ˈɡet.ɪŋ səm ˈpʊʃ.bæk/
People are expressing resistance, objections, or disagreement with a plan or idea; facing opposition or criticism.
We're getting some pushback from the stakeholders on the new pricing strategy. Management is getting some pushback on the proposed changes to the work schedule.
I just want to run through a few updates.
/aɪ dʒʌst wɒnt tə rʌn θruː ə fjuː ʌpˈdeɪts/
A statement indicating an intention to quickly review or summarize some recent developments or changes.
I just want to run through a few updates before we finish the meeting. I just want to run through a few updates on the project status.
All good here.
/ɔːl ɡʊd hɪər/
An informal expression meaning everything is fine, going well, or proceeding as expected; everything is satisfactory.
How's the project progressing? All good here. The new software installation is complete — all good here!
Quick one — …
/kwɪk wʌn/
A phrase used to introduce a brief comment, question, or point that won't take much time; a short item to discuss.
Quick one — has anyone seen the latest sales figures? Quick one — we need to confirm the meeting time for next week.
Works for me.
/wɜːks fɔː miː/
An expression of agreement or acceptance; indicating that a proposed plan, time, or arrangement is acceptable to you.
Shall we meet at 2pm tomorrow? Works for me. The new project timeline works for me if that's what everyone else agrees to.
Where do things stand on your end?
/weər duː θɪŋz stænd ɒn jɔːr end/
A question asking about the current status or progress of something from someone else's perspective or area of responsibility.
Where do things stand on your end with the client negotiations? I'd like to know where things stand on your end before we make a final decision.
We may need to reshuffle things.
/wiː meɪ niːd tə rɪˈʃʌf.əl θɪŋz/
A suggestion that we might need to reorganize, rearrange, or reconsider the current plan or structure; to make changes to how things are organized.
With the new budget cuts, we may need to reshuffle things to prioritize essential projects. If the deadline changes, we may need to reshuffle things on the team.

UNIT 7 - Career Development & Resilience

to bring the plans to a halt
/brɪŋ ðə plænz tə ə hɔːlt/
To stop or pause something completely; to cause plans or activities to stop suddenly.
The unexpected funding cuts brought the plans to a halt. The pandemic brought the expansion plans to a halt.
to serve as a wake-up call
/səːv əz ə ˈweɪk ʌp kɔːl/
To be a signal that alerts or reminds someone of something important; to make someone realize a serious problem or issue.
The poor sales figures served as a wake-up call for the team. His failure in the interview served as a wake-up call to study harder.
to find one's feet
/faɪnd wʌnz fiːt/
To become comfortable or confident in a new situation; to adapt and begin performing well in a new role or environment.
After a few months, the new employee began to find her feet in the company. It took time to find my feet in the new position.
without a hint of hesitation or fear
/wɪðˈaʊt ə hɪnt əv ˌhez.ɪˈteɪ.ʃən ɔː fɪər/
Showing complete confidence and courage; doing something with certainty and no doubt or worry.
She volunteered for the challenging project without a hint of hesitation or fear. He accepted the responsibility without a hint of hesitation or fear.
to set off with a clear mind
/set ɒf wɪð ə klɪər maɪnd/
To begin something with a focused perspective and positive attitude; to start a task or journey mentally prepared.
Before the important meeting, she took time to meditate to set off with a clear mind. The team set off with a clear mind to tackle the project.
to be briefed about
/biː ˈbriːfd əˈbaʊt/
To receive essential information or instruction about something; to be given a brief summary of key details.
All employees need to be briefed about the new safety procedures. I was briefed about the project goals during the meeting.
to settle in
/ˈset.əl ɪn/
To become comfortable and adjusted to a new place, role, or situation; to establish yourself in a new environment.
The new manager is starting to settle in after two weeks. It takes time for employees to settle in when they first join a company.
to get by
/ɡet baɪ/
To manage or cope with something, often with minimal resources; to survive or succeed despite difficulties.
On a tight budget, the team had to get by with limited resources. We can get by with the current staff, though we could use more help.
to compensate for something
/ˈkɒm.pen.seɪt fɔː ˈsʌm.θɪŋ/
To provide something that makes up for a loss or weakness; to balance out a negative aspect with something positive.
The excellent customer service compensates for the higher prices. We will compensate for the project delay with additional resources.
nerve-racking
/ˈnɜːv ræk.ɪŋ/
Very stressful or anxiety-inducing; causing nervous tension or worry.
The final presentation was nerve-racking for everyone on the team. Job interviews can be nerve-racking, especially for new graduates.
to make up for
/meɪk ʌp fɔː/
To compensate for something negative or lost; to do something good to offset a failure or mistake.
The team worked overtime to make up for lost time. I want to make up for my poor performance by delivering excellent results this quarter.
